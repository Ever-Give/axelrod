Nestled deep in the valley untouched by civilization was a small castle, Castle Meadpark, a stone tower house four floors tall, guarding a wooden arched bridge across the river and a scattering of thatched serf cottages in the surrounding acres. For two decades now the valley had been under the rule of the local baron and his wife who ruled their modest, if remote, estate fairly and efficiently for the most part, the capital never heard much from estates that remote anyway except for the annual tribute of grain.

It just so happened that this year it was my job to oversee the transport of the grain. I had, of course, done that before, just never quite this remote, this far from the capital. When my wife, Una, heard the news she was ecstatic. “This means the king trusts you to safely move it from a place as remote as Meadpark, you’re moving up in the world” she said.

It is customary to bring your wife with you on official business; it’s often said that you cannot function without your better half to bounce ideas off. I suppose it makes sense but I am still worried about her on such a long journey.

There will be long spans of time between inns and their, how should I phrase it, associated conveniences. Although it is highly emasculating to admit so, my wife has the ability to postpone her calls of nature for longer than me. Regardless of how much she drinks and how little I do on any day she always uses her commode less often than me. Needless to say, one should never take care of their animalistic needs in the presence of others, especially in front of gentry, so neither of us have seen the other take relief even though we have been married for three years. To this day I do not know how she manages to last so long at feasts, where wine, stout and mead flow like rivers without ‘going outside for some air’, even as the courtiers, noblewomen and even the hosts attempt to subtly scurry away often with their hands between their legs.

Many times already on this journey I would rush into an inn when the coach stops for meals to use their … comforts, thankful for the dark colour of my trousers while she only seemed to take care of her needs twice a day, once in the morning and once in the evening, like clockwork. In fact this trek was the only time I ever did see her desperate but thats a story for another time.
