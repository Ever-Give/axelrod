The Siege of Mandalore had been a marathon of fire and steel, but as the moons of the planet rose over the shattered skyline of Sundari, the battle entered its most claustrophobic phase. For Ahsoka Tano, the conflict had narrowed from the grand strategy of a planetary invasion to the desperate, oxygen-starved reality of a collapsed tunnel system.

She lay pinned in the dark, the weight of a thousand tons of Mandalorian duracrete and decorative stone settling just inches above her spine. A secondary explosion in the ventilation shafts had triggered a localized collapse, burying the access corridor she had been using to flank Maul’s shadow-collective guards.

Ahsoka’s breathing was shallow and rhythmic, a practiced Jedi technique to conserve the thinning air. But as the silence of the tomb settled around her, a different, more primal sensation began to override her survival instincts.

It had been nearly fourteen hours since she had last seen a refresher on the Tribunal. Fourteen hours of high-impact sprinting, acrobatic leaps that defied gravity, and the intense, dehydrating heat of lightsaber combat. During the height of the adrenaline surge, her body had suppressed its basic functions, channeling every ounce of energy into the Force. But now, in the forced stillness of her stone prison, the bill was coming due.

The pressure in her lower abdomen was no longer a distraction; it was a physical entity, a white-hot coal of urgency that throbbed with every heartbeat.

Ahsoka shifted her hips instinctively, but the movement was a mistake. Her "Siege of Mandalore" outfit, a gift from the 332nd, was a masterpiece of tactical layering—and a logistical fortress. The high-waisted compression leggings were designed to act as a second skin, providing muscular support for the grueling acrobatics of Form V combat. Over them sat the thick, navy-blue tunic, tucked firmly into the waistband and secured by a heavy, multi-layered fabric sash. Finally, the industrial-grade leather utility belt, laden with her dual lightsaber hilts and pouches of tech, was cinched so tight it felt like a structural part of her skeleton.

In this confined space, she couldn't even reach the buckle. Her arms were pinned forward, her fingers brushing against the cold handles of her lightsabers, which were useless in a collapse this unstable—igniting them would likely melt the support beams and bring the rest of the ceiling down on her head.

Focus, Tano, she hissed through gritted teeth, her forehead pressed against the cold durasteel floor. The Force is your ally. Control your body. Be the stone.

She tried to enter a meditative trance, reaching for the serenity that Master Plo Koon had taught her when she was just a child. She visualized a calm, still pond, the water perfectly level. But the metaphor betrayed her. The "pond" in her mind was overflowing, its banks bursting under the pressure of a torrential storm.

A sharp cramp seized her midsection, making her gasp. The involuntary contraction of her core muscles sent a wave of agonizing pressure through her bladder. She squeezed her eyes shut, her knuckles turning white as she gripped the handles of her sabers. Her knees were pressed together with such force they felt fused, her toes curled inside her boots until they cramped.

The suit, which had felt like armor hours ago, now felt like a trap. The thick, faux-suede fabric of the tunic and the non-breathable synthetic of the leggings didn't allow for any air circulation. The heat of her own body, trapped within the layers, only exacerbated the sensation. She could feel the heavy leather belt digging into her hip bone, right where the pressure was most intense, acting like a cruel thumb pressing down on a bruise.

"Rex... come in," she whispered, her voice cracking with the effort of holding back. "Rex... I’m in the sub-level 4... access pipe. Please... respond."

The comms unit on her wrist crackled with nothing but the hiss of static. The depth of the collapse was too great for the short-range signal.

She was alone. She was a Jedi Commander. She was the one who had walked away from the Order, who had faced down the Dark Side, who had led thousands of men into the mouth of hell. And yet, she was currently being brought to her knees by the most mundane of human needs.

Another hour passed in agonizing silence. The air grew warmer, or perhaps it was just the feverish intensity of her own physical struggle. Her muscles were beginning to tremble with fatigue. The "Force Hold" she was attempting to maintain—a desperate mental gatekeeping of her own anatomy—was failing. The dark side of the Force felt close, feeding on her frustration, her embarrassment, and her mounting desperation. It whispered that if she just let go, the pain would stop.

No, she thought, a tear of pure frustration leaking from the corner of her eye. I can hold it. I have to.

But the body has its limits, even for a Force-sensitive.

The final surge came without warning. A distant rumble—perhaps a heavy artillery strike on the surface or a rescue team using a seismic charge—shook the ground beneath her. The vibration traveled through the durasteel floor and straight into her spine.

Ahsoka’s breath hitched. A violent, involuntary spasm rocked her entire body as the shockwave hit. For one long, terrifying second, she fought it, her teeth bared in a silent scream of effort. She reached for the Force one last time, trying to brace the muscles that were failing her, but the physical reality of fourteen hours of suppression was a tide that no Jedi could turn back.

The dam broke.

The sensation was a sudden, searing rush of warmth that was at once a profound relief and a crushing, soul-deep humiliation. In the absolute darkness of the crawlspace, the heat spread rapidly, trapped instantly by the high-waisted compression leggings. Because the fabric was designed to be form-fitting and moisture-wicking for sweat, it didn't just let the liquid pass; it absorbed it, pulling the warmth tight against her skin.

Ahsoka let out a long, shuddering sob, her forehead dropping onto the cold floor. The "involved process" of her outfit had made this outcome inevitable the moment she was pinned, but that realization did nothing to dull the sting of the moment. The heavy fabric of her tunic, tucked into the waistband, began to pull the moisture upward through capillary action, the sash becoming damp and heavy.

She lay there for a long time, the silence of the tunnel returning, punctuated only by her own ragged, shamed breathing. The relief of the physical pressure was gone, replaced by a cold, clinging discomfort that felt like a brand. She felt smaller than she ever had—not like a warrior, but like a lost child trapped in a cage of her own making.

I’m sorry, Master, she whispered into the dark, though she wasn't sure if she was talking to Anakin or Plo Koon.

The heat of the accident began to fade, replaced by a chilling dampness as the Mandalore air circulated through the gaps in the rubble. The compression leggings, designed to keep her muscles warm and ready for action, were now a cold, wet vice. Every time she shifted her legs to try and find a more comfortable position, the damp fabric reminded her of her failure.

Another thirty minutes passed before the sound of metal on stone reached her.

"Commander! I see her boots! Over here!"

It was Rex. The light of a tactical torch cut through the dust, blinding her for a moment. She heard the whine of a hydraulic jack as the troopers began to lift the heavy slab of concrete from her legs.

"Ahsoka! Can you hear me?" Rex’s voice was filled with a frantic, brotherly concern.

"I'm here," she said, but her voice was a hollowed-out version of itself. She didn't move. She didn't reach out for his hand. She stayed pressed against the floor, her heart hammering against her ribs.

"We’ve got you, ma’am. Just a few more seconds," a trooper named Jesse said, grunting as he helped guide the slab upward.

As the weight was finally lifted, Rex reached in, grabbing her shoulders to pull her out of the narrow space. As he dragged her into the light of the larger corridor, the reality of her condition became visible. The dark blue fabric of her tunic and the charcoal leggings were heavily discolored, the moisture having spread through the absorbent layers of the sash and belt.

Rex paused for a fraction of a second, his tactical helmet tilting as his sensors likely picked up the sudden change in temperature and humidity around her. The other troopers went silent.

Ahsoka couldn't look at them. She kept her eyes fixed on the floor, her hands shaking as she reached for her lightsabers, trying to find some shred of her former dignity to wrap around herself like a cloak.

Rex didn't say a word. He didn't make a joke, and he didn't ask if she was hurt. With the quiet, intuitive grace of a man who had spent years in the trenches, he stepped forward, unfastening his own grey pauldron-cape and draping it over her shoulders. The heavy fabric fell down her back, covering the dampness of her tunic and the tell-tale stains on her leggings.

"The air support is waiting, Commander," Rex said, his voice as steady as a rock. "Let’s get you back to the ship so you can... prepare for the final debrief."

"Thank you, Rex," she whispered, her voice barely audible.

"Don't mention it, ma’am,” he replied, placing a steadying hand on her arm as he helped her stand. "It was a long mission. The longest I can remember."

As they walked through the ruined halls of Sundari, Ahsoka gripped the edges of the trooper’s cape, pulling it tight around her waist. The war was almost over. The Siege was won. But as she felt the cold, wet weight of the suit beneath the cape, she knew that some battles were fought in the heart, and the scars they left were far more enduring than those made by a lightsaber.

She had survived the collapse, but she had lost something of the invincibility she had cultivated. She was human. She was vulnerable. And as they stepped out into the cold Mandalorian night, she decided that perhaps that was a lesson she needed to learn before the galaxy changed forever.
