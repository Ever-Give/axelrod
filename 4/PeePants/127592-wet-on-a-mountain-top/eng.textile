It's a long story how this came to be, but I was alone in Brașov, and would be for a couple of days.

The central European landscape could be truly fascinating for anyone not familiar with it. Mile upon mile of dead flat plain, but here the flatness seems rudely interrupted by huge and steep hills or mountains. Above the town to the south towered the Southern Carpathians, the closest of the hills having a large sign near the summit, "BRASOV" it proclaimed.

What to do with the day. The hotel breakfast was fair, the weather was fair, a good day for a long walk, and the hills seemed to call. Reaching the edge of the woods at the base of the hill a sign warned of bears, wolves and lynx, but this was evidently a well used footpath winding up the hill so any danger must have been slight.

Working my way up I noticed how I'd let my peak condition slide a little as I breathed heavily and stopped a few times for breath, but I made it to the top and lingered a while by the 8 foot high letters of the sign. There were only a few people around and I wandered further along the ridge. I'd noticed that down in the town there had been a gentle breeze but up here, strangely, the air seemed perfectly still. I moved off the path into the dense, healthy, sloping woodland and found a fallen tree which made a perfect bench to sit on. I sat here a while looking down to the town through the trees. There was silence. No, almost silence. No sound could be heard from the town below, any other people were too far away to be seen or heard, and it occurred to me I was hearing only the sad and gentle sounds made by falling leaves.

It felt strangely beautiful to be so at one with nature.
